# Private Parking Fees in Poland - Brief Case and Legal Situation Summary

Date: 2026-05-10  
Status: working summary based on the Polish analysis dated 2026-05-08  
Scope: private car parks near shops, service facilities, residential estates and other private properties

## Purpose

This note summarises the Polish working analysis on claims for fees, penalties or other charges imposed by private parking operators. It is intended as a short English-language overview of the legal issue, the main case-law trends and the practical arguments available to both parking operators and drivers or vehicle owners.

The note does not concern public paid parking zones on public roads. Case law on public parking fees is relevant only by analogy and mainly to distinguish public-law parking charges from civil-law claims raised by private parking operators.

## Legal Situation

Polish courts generally analyse private parking disputes as civil-law matters. A private parking operator does not issue a public-law fine or an administrative penalty. A notice to pay issued by such an operator is a private civil claim which must be proven in civil proceedings if disputed.

The most common legal framework is a contract for the lease or use of a parking space. Courts often accept that entering a private car park and occupying a space may amount to implied acceptance of the parking regulations. The relevant legal basis is typically Article 659 of the Polish Civil Code, with the parking regulations treated as standard contractual terms under Article 384 of the Civil Code.

However, the person liable is usually the actual user of the parking space, not automatically the vehicle owner, possessor or lessee. The operator must prove that the defendant was the person who used the parking space or otherwise became bound by the parking regulations.

## Main Case-Law Trends

The key line of cases concerns evidentiary problems. In several judgments from Szczecin, courts dismissed operators' claims because the operator proved that the vehicle was parked but did not prove that the defendant was the driver or actual user of the parking space. Registration plates, vehicle ownership data and photos of the vehicle were not enough to establish contractual liability of the owner.

A second important issue is the legal character of the fee. Some courts treat the charge stated in the parking regulations as a contractual fee or remuneration for using the parking space under specified conditions, for example after exceeding a free parking period. In that approach, the charge is not necessarily a contractual penalty.

A newer judgment from the District Court Katowice-Wschod, 24 October 2024, case VII GC 455/24, takes a stricter approach where the fee is constructed as a penalty for failure to pay the basic parking meter fee. The court held that a contractual penalty cannot be reserved for non-performance of a monetary obligation under Article 483 of the Civil Code. In that case, the relevant clauses were considered invalid under Article 58 of the Civil Code.

Another significant line concerns wheel clamps. In the judgment of the Regional Court in Katowice, 23 June 2017, case IV Ca 314/17, the court indicated that the use of wheel clamps by private entities is highly problematic without a clear statutory basis, because it interferes with ownership and is a form of coercive enforcement.

## Selected Key Authorities

- District Court Szczecin-Prawobrzeze i Zachod in Szczecin, 14 September 2016, III C 557/16: the operator failed to prove that the vehicle owner was the actual parking user; the claim was dismissed.
- District Court Szczecin-Prawobrzeze i Zachod in Szczecin, 6 October 2016, III C 1373/15: a private parking regulation cannot automatically impose liability on the vehicle owner for the conduct of another person.
- Regional Court in Katowice, 23 June 2017, IV Ca 314/17: payment made after a wheel clamp was imposed was successfully challenged; private wheel clamping was treated as legally problematic.
- District Court Szczecin-Centrum in Szczecin, 14 March 2019, III C 1987/18: the claim against a vehicle lessee was dismissed because the recording indicated that another person used the vehicle.
- District Court Katowice-Wschod in Katowice, 24 October 2024, VII GC 455/24: a contractual penalty for failure to pay a monetary parking fee was held invalid.

## Consumer Protection Context

Decisions of the President of the Polish Office of Competition and Consumer Protection (UOKiK) are also relevant. They do not automatically decide individual parking disputes, but they support arguments concerning unfair communication, complaint handling and additional administrative or collection costs.

Important UOKiK decisions include:

- EuroPark, decision RLU 11/2014: UOKiK questioned additional administrative fees and clauses allowing the operator to add debt collection costs.
- TD System, decision RKT-3/2021: UOKiK found unfair the refusal to cancel additional fee notices where consumers were store customers, had a parking ticket and proof of shopping, and the free parking time had not expired.
- APCOA Parking Polska, decision RKR-4/2022: UOKiK questioned misleading information about complaint channels, collection costs and complaint handling deadlines.

## Practical Implications

For parking operators, the key issue is evidence. They should be able to prove the applicable regulations, proper signage, the terms binding the driver, the legal basis and amount of the fee, and the identity of the person actually using the parking space.

For drivers and vehicle owners, the main defensive arguments are: lack of proof that the defendant was the driver, lack of clear signage or applicable regulations, invalidity of a contractual penalty for non-payment of a monetary fee, excessive or unjustified additional costs, and the civil-law nature of the notice to pay.

In any concrete case, the decisive documents will usually be the parking regulations, photos of the signage as of the date of parking, evidence of the vehicle's use, the notice to pay, proof of delivery, and any complaint correspondence.

## Working Conclusion

The current Polish case-law does not reject private parking charges as such. It does, however, require operators to prove the contractual basis of the claim and the defendant's personal liability. The strongest defence usually arises when the operator only proves ownership or possession of the vehicle, but not the identity of the actual driver or parking user.

The classification of the charge is also crucial. If it is a genuine parking fee for use of the space, the dispute focuses on contract formation, signage and amount. If it is in substance a penalty for not paying a monetary fee, Article 483 of the Civil Code may provide a strong invalidity argument.

This summary remains a working note and should be verified against the current text of the relevant judgments, regulations and UOKiK decisions before being used in litigation or client advice.
